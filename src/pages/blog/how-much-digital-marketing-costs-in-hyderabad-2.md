## What drives digital marketing cost in Hyderabad

Several factors shape the price you pay: the scope of services, the experience level of the team, and the local market dynamics in Hyderabad. A full‑service agency that handles strategy, creative, and analytics will charge more than a specialist freelancer who only runs Google Ads. Seasonal demand, competition in your niche, and the need for multilingual content (Telugu, Hindi, English) also move the needle. If you're wondering how much digital marketing costs in Hyderabad, start by mapping the services you actually need.

## Typical price ranges by service

- **SEO (monthly retainer)**: ₹30,000 – ₹1,20,000 depending on keyword difficulty and reporting depth.
- **PPC management (Google/Meta)**: 10‑20 % of ad spend, with a minimum fee of ₹15,000/month.
- **Content marketing (blog + social)**: ₹20,000 – ₹80,000 per month for a consistent calendar.
- **Social media management**: ₹15,000 – ₹60,000/month for community management and paid amplification.
- **Website development & CRO**: one‑off ₹1,00,000 – ₹5,00,000 plus optional retainer.

## In‑house vs agency vs freelancer

Building an in‑house team gives control but adds salaries, tools, and training overhead — often ₹8‑12 lakh per year for a modest three‑person squad. Agencies bundle expertise and tools, typically costing ₹2‑5 lakh per month for a mid‑size account. Freelancers are the cheapest entry point (₹10,000‑₹40,000/month) but you manage coordination and quality yourself.

## How to budget without surprises

1. Define clear KPIs before asking for proposals.
2. Request itemised quotes so you see exactly what each rupee covers.
3. Set a contingency of 10‑15 % for scope changes.
4. Agree on reporting cadence and ownership of assets up front.

## Quick checklist for getting quotes

- List required channels (search, social, email, video).
- Note target geography and language needs.
- Specify current tech stack (CRM, analytics, CMS).
- Ask for case studies relevant to your industry (no names needed).
- Confirm contract length, exit clauses, and IP ownership.
